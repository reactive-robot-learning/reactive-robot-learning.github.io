Closing the Loop

Reactive Execution for Behavior Foundation Models

Program Challenges Contributions

Why reactive execution?

Pre-trained robot foundation models have expanded the range of manipulation tasks robots can perform, but are mostly used in settings where the environment changes little between observation and action. Real-world tasks are dynamic: industrial robots need high throughput, robots working alongside people must respond safely to unexpected changes, and catching or striking requires precise timing.

Yet learned policies (such as vision-language-action and World-Action models) often update their actions much more slowly than sensors provide new information or low-level controllers operate, leaving robots acting on outdated information. This workshop asks:

What must change across perception, learning, inference, and execution for robots to work faster and respond reliably to changes in their environment?

Failure videos

A separate track accepts short failure videos of robots in dynamic settings. Please include the system information: sensing rate, inference latency, replanning frequency, and controller rate. Selected clips open the day in the Failure Gallery, where the audience diagnoses them in a live poll.

Two complementary challenges

Fast perception and reaction in a competitive task, and adaptation to changing conditions in everyday manipulation with moving objects. At the workshop, the Air Hockey organizers report the results together with the top teams, and the top five DOM teams discuss in a panel what worked and what did not.

Robot Air Hockey Challenge 2027

Robot Air Hockey Challenge 2027

The deployed policy receives 50 Hz RGB-D observations and robot proprioception instead of privileged motion-capture state. Teams must extract and predict task-relevant state from vision and react in time.

Released to teams: synchronized human–human play and human-to-robot teleoperation data, robot actions and rewards, motion-capture ground truth for puck and pusher, and a physics simulator. Any approach is welcome: imitation and reinforcement learning, planning, world models, explicit perception pipelines, end-to-end visuomotor learning, or hybrids.

Everyday manipulation · moving objects

Dynamic Object Manipulation Challenge

Click for details

Dynamic Object Manipulation Challenge

Based on a selected subset of the DOM benchmark. Robots grasp and place objects in a scene with moving objects. By varying object motion, friction and other physical properties, the challenge tests whether robots adapt their actions as conditions change.

Learned policies, perception and control pipelines, and combinations of the two are all welcome.
